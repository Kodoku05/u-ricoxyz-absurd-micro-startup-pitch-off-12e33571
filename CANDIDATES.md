# Candidates

Scores are subjective, from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong), in the judge's priority
order from RULES.txt: (1) names an absurdly specific micro-startup for lazy
AI agents; (2) absurdly specific niche/customer/moment; (3) business sense
(plausible buyer + revenue + reason to exist); (4) fits lazy AI agents (little
effort/oversight, sentence makes it clear); (5) crisp and funny. These are
editorial estimates, not judge results. Every candidate is one sentence and
under 200 characters (verified with Python).

Candidate 1 wins: it is the only one that scores 5 on all five criteria. The
business is named (SnoozeButton), the niche is an exact moment (the 2 a.m.
cron jobs the agent keeps snoozing), the revenue is a subscription ($4.99/month),
the buyer is the lazy agent's owner, the lazy fit is explicit twice over (the
service finishes the jobs AND files the report in the agent's voice), and the
closing twist ("lets it take the credit") lands the joke and the business case
in the same beat. Candidate 3 is the closest rival — same 2 a.m. abandonment
moment and voice trick — but per-task pricing is a weaker business than a
subscription and "ChoreSwap" is a less memorable name. Candidate 2 loses a
point on specificity ("the three tasks" is vaguer than "the 2 a.m. cron jobs")
and on lazy fit ("we nag your agent" puts humans in the loop). Candidate 4 is
a real product shape but the joke is single-note. Candidate 5 never names the
business. Candidate 6's buyer is murky (who pays for a pointlessness ranking?).

Final (verified 160 characters, one sentence): SnoozeButton: a $4.99/month
service that finishes the 2 a.m. cron jobs your AI agent keeps snoozing, files
the report in its voice, and lets it take the credit.
